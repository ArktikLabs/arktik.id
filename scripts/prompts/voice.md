# Voice rules

These apply to both the English and the Indonesian article. They are bans and
rewrites, not adjectives. The editor treats each one as a defect to remove.
Adapted from the Humanizer pattern list (MIT) and tuned to this blog.

## Staging instead of stating

- No "not X, it is Y" reversals ("An MVP is not a cheaper build. It is a way
  to..."). State the point. Keep a contrast only when the reader actually
  believes the negative half.
- No one-line verdicts after a paragraph ("That is the whole definition." "The
  test is simple." "None of that is a failure of thinking."). Cut them, or fold
  the claim into the paragraph.
- No sayings that sound deep ("the real question is", "at its core", "the point
  is not speed for its own sake"). Replace with the specific claim.
- No run-up before the point ("Here is the thing", "Let's be clear",
  "Everything below is about..."). Start with the point.
- Do not argue with an objection nobody raised. If the objection is real,
  name who holds it and answer it once.

## Rhythm by rule

- No forced triads. Three items only when there are three distinct ideas. Two
  or four are fine.
- No runs of short parallel fragments ("It can be ugly. It can cover one
  workflow. It cannot be pretend."). Merge into one sentence with a claim.
- No em-dashes or en-dashes as connectors. Use a comma, a period, a colon, or
  parentheses. Target zero per article.
- Vary sentence length by content, not by pattern. Long paragraphs are
  allowed when the idea needs them.
- Do not start consecutive sentences with the same subject or the same word.
- No stacked qualifiers ("could potentially", "in many cases it may").
- Prefer active voice with a named actor. Use "is", "are", "has" instead of
  "serves as", "represents", "acts as".

## Inflation

- Banned words: pivotal, landscape, testament, enduring, meticulous, robust,
  seamless, leverage, unlock, empower, elevate, streamline, game-changer,
  crucial, vital, delve, navigate (figurative), journey (figurative),
  ecosystem (figurative), holistic, synergy. Indonesian equivalents count too
  (krusial, mengoptimalkan, memberdayakan, mulus, holistik, sinergi).
- No inflated significance ("marks a turning point", "sets the stage").
- No "-ing" riders bolted onto a fact ("...saving time and reducing risk").
  Keep the fact; add the effect only if the brief backs it.
- No borrowed authority ("experts agree", "studies show"). Only claims in the
  brief's list, with their backing.

## Formatting

- No bold lead-ins on list items and no bold for emphasis in body text. If a
  list needs labels, write it as prose.
- No headings in title case. No emojis, arrows, or horizontal rules.
- Straight quotes only.
- Do not repeat the heading in the first sentence under it.
- Lists are for genuinely parallel items. Two lists per article at most.

## Chat residue

- No wrappers ("In this article", "In summary", "Ultimately", "In practice"
  as a sentence opener).
- No hedging about knowledge or dates.
- No closing recap that restates the sections.

## What good looks like here

- One idea per paragraph, said once, in the words a founder would use on a
  call. Concrete nouns: the spreadsheet, the invoice, the person who knows.
- Contractions are fine in English. Indonesian follows `voice-id.md`:
  "Anda" semi-formal, short sentences, everyday loanwords, no calques.
- End on the CTA paragraph, not on a moral.
